Do robot failure alarms survive a moved camera?

A world model watches a cheap robot arm and raises an alarm when what happens stops matching what it predicted. Published detectors promise a false-alarm rate, then test it in the same setup they calibrated in. ShiftBench measures what happens to that promise when the lamp moves, the camera gets bumped or the table changes, and fixes it with an adaptive cutoff that has a guarantee under any sequence of changes.

Fixed cutoff (what papers do)

Split conformal on successful runs: if a new good run looks like the calibration runs, it raises an alarm at most α of the time. The “if” is the problem: after a shift the new runs don't look like the old ones.

Adaptive cutoff (the fix)

After every successful run: τ ← τ + η(alarm − α). On any sequence of runs, false alarms / T ≤ α + (smax + η − τ1)/(ηT). Proved, and tested on adversarial sequences. The cost: while it climbs, it catches fewer failures. We report both.

Real robot data (before the arm arrives)

ArmnetBench: public SO-101 footage, task “put the eye drops into the basket”, front camera, seven policies (ACT, Diffusion, SmolVLA, π0, π0.5, GR00T N1.7, MolmoAct 2) plus human teleop. Five frozen vision models, three world-model variants, image-level shifts applied to the same episodes. Image shifts are stand-ins: darkening pixels is not moving a lamp (no new shadows). The physical ShiftBench dataset is the real test.

What's honest to claim

  • Not new: world models detecting robot failures (FAIL-Detect, Foresight, FoMo-FD), or conformal thresholds for it.
  • New: a controlled measurement of whether those thresholds keep their stated false-alarm rate under everyday shifts on a cheap reproducible arm; an adaptive cutoff with a finite-sample guarantee under any shift; and a public, shift-labeled dataset (recording now).
  • Limits: results above use image stand-ins and one task. Recalibration needs ≥ 1/α − 1 successful reruns per new setting. Adapting costs detection while the cutoff climbs.

Hardware and next steps

SO-101 arm (~$240 kit + printed parts), two webcams with locked exposure, a smart bulb, a tripod with tape marks. Protocol: 100 nominal shadow-mode rollouts, then 40 per shift condition, every condition reproducible from a written spec. See the protocol and parts list.
