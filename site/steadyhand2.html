SteadyHand 2: a hand that looks before it grabs

A wrist camera guesses which grip the object affords. Muscle signals (EMG) say when to grab and which grip. The hand moves only when both agree with enough confidence; otherwise it holds still. The misfire promise comes from SteadyHand's conformal and adaptive cutoffs.

Try it: shift the electrodes, dim the light

Simulated users (not people). Cutoffs are set on clean data to a 5% misfire target, then the signals degrade. SteadyHand 1 measured the real version of the EMG failure: 46% misfires at LibEMG's 0.90 cutoff after a 45° electrode shift.

Misfire = the hand moved when the user didn't want to, or with the wrong grip. Correct grabs = share of intended grabs executed with the right grip.

Why agreement helps

One signal going confidently wrong can't move the hand by itself, because the other has to agree. The price: when either signal is weak, the hand moves less often. The paper's job is to measure that trade with real wrist video and real EMG, at a stated misfire rate.
